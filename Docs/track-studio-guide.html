Track Studio

Editing TTA_Sandbox in the Unity Editor, getting it back into the game, and making a new map from scratch.

About the pictures. The panels below are hand-built mockups of the Unity UI, not captured screenshots. Every label in them is taken from the source — the [MenuItem] strings in Assets/Editor/TrackStudio/ and the button text in TrackStudioWindow.cs — so they match what is actually on screen, but the pixels are drawn here rather than photographed.

TTA_Sandbox is already a registered scene track, so there is no import step in the sense of a file being copied anywhere. The scene is the track: it lives in the project, it ships inside the build, and the game loads it by name. Editing it and "putting it back" is just open, change, re-bake, save.

Part 1 — Editing TTA_Sandbox

1. Open the scene

In the Project window: Assets/TinyTorqueAssets/Scenes/TTA_Sandbox.unity — double-click it.

Do not use Tools/AIHWSim/Create Map Debug Scene to get to this scene. That menu item creates the scene and will replace it with an empty one — terrains, painted surfaces and baked lighting included. It now asks for confirmation first; say Cancel if you ever see that dialog by accident.

2. Open the Track Studio window

The window is the dashboard for whatever scene is currently open. Everything in the numbered menu is also a button in here, plus a live read-out of what the scene has.

3. Change things

Terrain sculpting, ProBuilder geometry, props and lighting are all just normal Unity work — nothing in Track Studio cares how the scene got its shape. The parts that are track data:

To changeDo thisThen
Road shape or widthSelect the SplineContainer, use Unity's Spline toolRe-bake — step 2
Surface / gripSurface Brush, paint onto terrain or meshesnothing
Gate positionsMove the marker, then Snap to road in its inspectorRenumber if you added one
Spawn pointsMove / add TrackSpawnMarkersnothing
Sky, fog, ambienceScene lighting, plus the descriptor's ambience fieldsnothing
The re-bake is the step people forget. Moving a spline does not move the road mesh, the bot corridor or the baked racing line — those are all baked artifacts. A racing line baked against an older spline is the single most likely real failure, which is why the validator explicitly checks the line against the corridor it was solved in.

4. Surface Brush

Tools ▸ Track Studio ▸ Surface Brush. Pick a floor type from the palette, set a radius, paint in the Scene view. It writes terrain alphamaps and mesh SurfaceTags through the same click, so you do not choose a target — it paints whatever you are pointing at.

The palette shows each floor's frictionMult for a reason: anything under 0.90 also counts as off track for arcade track limits. So painting grass gives you a track-limits penalty zone for free, and painting a whole map in something at 1.00 means track limits never trigger anywhere on it.

5. Save, and you're done

Ctrl+S. That is the "import back into the game" step — TTA_Sandbox is already in SceneTrackCatalog.All and in Build Settings, so the game picks up the saved scene the next time it loads it. Press Play in the editor to drive it, or make a build.

The sandbox is registered as FreeRoam, and FreeRoam tracks are filtered out of the race picker — the same convention Torque Falls follows, since there is nothing to race on a map with no finish line. You reach it by opening the scene and pressing Play. Give it a finish line and a dense set of checkpoints, flip the descriptor's kind to Circuit, and it appears in the menu as ▣ Sandbox.

6. Check it (optional, but cheap)

Validate this scene in the window, or headlessly across every registered track:

"E:\Unity Hub\Editor\6000.1.15f1\Editor\Unity.exe" -batchmode -quit -projectPath "E:\EE Projects\Tiny_Torque\UnitySim" -executeMethod AIHWSim.TrackTools.TrackStudioValidator.Report -logFile trk.log

Then look for [TRK] RESULT in trk.log. Fourteen checks, including the two silent killers: checkpoint orders that are not a dense 0..n-1 run, and a stale racing line.

Part 2 — Making a new map

Seven steps. The first six are in the editor; the seventh is two lines of registration that are deliberately manual, because a track that is half-registered fails in a confusing way.

1. New scene, build the world

File ▸ New Scene, save it under Assets/Scenes/ with the name you want the track to have. Add terrain, geometry, lighting — whatever the map is.

2. Make it a track

Tools ▸ Track Studio ▸ 1. Make this scene a track. This adds a TrackDescriptor object, seeds one spawn marker, and — if the scene has terrain — creates a TerrainFloorTable guessed from the terrain layers actually present.

Review the guesses. They come from layer names, and a layer called "Rock" could reasonably be dirt, paving or obsidian. The validator only insists that every layer has a row, so a wrong guess is a wrong guess it will happily accept.

Select the descriptor and set kind: Circuit for a lap-timed track, FreeRoam for a playground. (Arena is refused — arena mode needs a floor slab to compute its playfield from, and a hand-authored scene has none.)

3. Add a road

Add spline road in the window drops a SplineContainer with a TrackSplineAuthoring component. Shape it with Unity's normal Spline tool — click to add knots, drag tangents. Width, banking and surface type are SplineData channels on the authoring component, so they are keyed along the curve rather than being one value for the whole road.

4. Bake the ribbon

2. Bake ribbon + corridor. Generates the road mesh, splits it into per-surface colliders with their own SurfaceTags, adds kerbs and optional walls, and bakes the centreline that bots follow.

5. Place gates

Add finish, then Add checkpoint as many times as you need. For each one: position it, then hit Snap to road in its inspector.

A gate is directional. It spans its own local X, and cars are counted as passing through when they cross travelling along its local +Z. A gate rotated by eye is the classic "my lap never counts and nothing in the console says why" bug — which is exactly what Snap to road exists to prevent.

Then 3. Renumber checkpoints. Orders must be a dense 0, 1, 2, … n-1: the lap timer only advances on an exact index match and refuses to count a lap until every checkpoint has been hit, so a single gap makes the track permanently un-lappable with no error message anywhere.

6. Paint, then bake a line

Surface Brush for grip. Then 4. Bake racing line to solve the ideal line inside the corridor and put a speed profile on it — the Scene view draws it coloured by speed, with apex markers and brake-zone bars. Optionally 5. Calibrate racing line (play mode), which drives it for three laps and fits four numbers describing the car (grip scale, acceleration, top speed, brake usage), and 6. Sector configurator to slice it into sectors with target times.

The baked line is analysis and visualization. Nothing in the shipped game reads it — bots still use their runtime heuristic, so baking a line does not change race feel or lap times.

7. Register it — both halves

Add a row to SceneTrackCatalog.All in Assets/Scripts/Track/SceneTrackCatalog.cs:

new Row { scene = "MyTrack", label = "My Track",
          kind = TrackPresets.TrackKind.Circuit },

And add the scene to File ▸ Build Profiles ▸ Scene List.

Both are required. A catalog row with no build entry is a picker item that loads a black screen — SceneManager.LoadScene simply cannot find a scene that is not in the build. The validator fails on one without the other for this reason.

Validate, and it is a track.

Part 3 — Spline roads, and what a "key" is

Converting a spline you already have

A road is just a SplineContainer with a TrackSplineAuthoring component next to it. Converting adds that component and changes nothing about your curve — same knots, same tangents, same position.

Select the spline, then Tools ▸ Track Studio ▸ Convert selected spline to road, or use the buttons that appear in the Track Studio window:

You already hit this one. A spline drawn before Track Studio usually has a SplineExtrude generating its own mesh. Baking a ribbon on top leaves two overlapping road surfaces competing for the same wheel raycasts — grip then depends on which collider wins the raycast, which reads as random. Disable or remove the SplineExtrude first, which is what you did.

What a channel is, and what a key is

A Unity spline stores position and tangents and nothing else. It has no idea how wide your road is, how much it leans, or what it's made of. Those three live on the authoring component as channels.

A channel starts empty, which means "use the single default value above, for the entire road." That is a perfectly good road — you never have to touch a channel.

A key pins a value at one point along the curve. Position runs 0 at the start of the spline to 1 at the end. Between two keys the value blends smoothly:

So "add a width key or two" just means: click Add key twice and set the two values, if you want the road to change width. Key counts behave like this:

KeysResult
0Constant, using the default field above the channel. The normal case.
1Still constant — at the key's value. Nothing to blend toward.
2+The value blends from key to key along the road.

The three channels

Width — metres, edge to edge. Straightforward.

Banking — degrees of lean. Positive drops the right edge, per SplineSpec.rollDeg.

Check the sign yourself before you build a whole track on it. Which way positive leans relative to the direction a corner turns was never confirmed against a driven car — it was flagged as unverified when this was designed and it still is. Set one key to something obvious like 15°, bake, and look at the road in the Scene view. A sign error makes banked corners drive slower than flat ones, which is a genuinely confusing bug to chase later.

Surface — which of the 18 TrackCatalog.Floors the road is made of, and the thing that decides grip. The dropdown shows each floor's grip value.

Surface blends, which is rarely what you want. The channel interpolates like the other two, so a key of Asphalt at 0.3 and Dirt at 0.7 gives you a smeared ramp through every floor id in between. For a hard edge — asphalt that becomes dirt at one line across the road — put two keys very close together: asphalt at 0.499, dirt at 0.501.

Worth knowing

  • Scene tracks cannot be opened in the in-game Track Builder. That builder edits TrackDesign tile maps, which are data; a scene track is a scene. Different track sources, both live.
  • Over LAN a scene track travels as a name, not as data — so everyone needs the same build. A client that does not have the scene is refused with a message rather than being dropped onto the classic oval and silently desynced.
  • The scene owns its own sky. The track scene loads first and TrackScene comes in additively on top, so your lighting, skybox, fog and baked lightmaps are the ones that survive.
  • Terrain is real physics now. Painted surfaces are baked to one floor id per texel when the track binds, so what you paint is what the tyres feel.
